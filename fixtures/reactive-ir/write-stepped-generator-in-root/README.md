# write-stepped-generator-in-root

**Claim (ADR 0521).** A generator stepped at its own call (`(function* () { … })().next()`) runs its first step on the stack of that call, under the owner of the code around it, so a write or an action in it is as legal as one written at the call: the write walk places the call, where a `createRoot` or `runWithOwner(owner)` body in a handler rejects it, and the handler, `untrack` or `runWithOwner(null)` does not. Before, the read walk's role at the call (the handler's) was passed through as legal, and the root between was skipped (38 `mx-recall` false cleans, contexts `pos-handler-root` and `pos-handler-owner`, layer `generator`). Violations (rc.17 throws `REACTIVE_WRITE_IN_OWNED_SCOPE`, `ACTION_CALLED_IN_OWNED_SCOPE` for the action, on the click): `HandlerRootSteppedWrite`, `HandlerRootSteppedAction`, `BodyRootSteppedWrite` (uncertifiable before), `HandlerRootExclusiveArms` (a `yield` in the other arm of an `if` cannot precede the write).

Legal (rc.17 silent): the generator stepped directly in the handler, under `untrack` or under `runWithOwner(null)`; a strict read in the handler's root (the window is open); a write after a straight-line `yield` or a body only `return()` steps (never run); an action body (ADR 0520, a generator the action owns, not one stepped at its call).

Uncertifiable: a write in a loop whose `yield` may precede it (`HandlerRootLoopYield`; rc.17 silent, the one step stops at the `yield`); the bound-owner twin (`HandlerOwnerSteppedWrite`; rc.17 throws), which every layer under `runWithOwner(boundOwner, …)` in a handler leaves unplaced, as the plain closure does (a recall gap this fixture records, not a claim).
